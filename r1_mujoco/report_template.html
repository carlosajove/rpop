Numbers for the set designer

Robot

  • Standing height 1.22 m; pelvis 0.73 m; shoulder axis 0.97 m.
  • Footprint: heel 5 cm behind to toe 13 cm ahead of the pelvis line; feet ~30 cm apart outside-to-outside.
  • Arm cannot cross the body midline: shoulder roll allows 13° inward, 142° outward. Right hand works the right half-space, left hand the left; the shared zone is the narrow band ±27 cm in front of the chest ({{OVL}} m³).

Hands (grasp point)

  • Height band {{ZMIN}}–{{ZMAX}} m. Nothing the hands must grab should sit below 0.5 m; put handles, hooks and table tops at 0.7–1.1 m.
  • Forward reach by height: {{R05}} m at 0.5 m, {{R07}} at 0.7, {{R09}} at 0.9, {{R11}} at 1.1, {{R13}} at 1.3 m.
  • Sideways: to 0.64 m on the hand's own side, only 0.24 m across. With the waist turned (±150°) either hand covers ±0.63 m and {{XMAXW}} m ahead.

Rake: can the head touch the floor?

Criterion: rake head within 5 cm of the floor, at least 30 cm ahead of the toes, handle inclined 25–60° (steeper is poking, flatter cannot pull). Footprint = floor area the head can be placed on from a standing position, one hand, collision-free. The grip angle is the angle between the handle and the fingers: 0° is a plain handle across the palm, 90° puts the handle along the forearm.

Why the grip matters. The R1 wrist has one axis (roll about the forearm). Anything held across the palm is therefore always perpendicular to the forearm, and getting a 1 m shaft down to the floor in front means holding the forearm up at an awkward angle that the shoulder rarely allows. An angled grip lets the shaft point down-forward while the forearm stays in its comfortable band. Same reason a two-hand grip on a straight shaft is so constrained.

  • Rigid two-hand grips exist only with the shaft running diagonally across the body (left hand high on the left, right hand low on the right, shaft yawed 30–60°), at hand heights 0.7–1.3 m, never with both hands low.
  • They are isolated poses: moving the shaft through a raking stroke leaves the feasible set almost immediately. Design for one rigid hand plus a loose second, or give the shaft two grips each angled to its own forearm.

Teleoperation check

xr_teleoperate's own R1 5-DoF IK (the code that will drive the real robot) was fed a scripted rake stroke and evaluated in the same MuJoCo model: 1.3 ms per solve, under 1 cm error inside the workspace, joint limits respected. Targets outside the workspace are pulled to the nearest reachable pose (11 cm error for a hand asked to go 0.48 m ahead at 0.55 m height), which is what the operator will feel as the robot "not following" low, far-forward hand positions. The full xr_teleoperate stack now runs against the MuJoCo model instead of the robot, so strokes can be practised in the Pico 4 before delivery.

Method and caveats

  • Model: Unitree r1_description/R1.urdf (same arms as r1_a5) compiled in MuJoCo 3.8; 12 leg joints removed at hip −0.20 / knee 0.42 / ankle −0.23 rad; pelvis raised so the soles touch the floor. Straight legs add 1.3 cm to every height.
  • Hands: BrainCo Revo 2 URDFs from xr_teleoperate mounted 14 cm along the wrist axis with Unitree's G1 convention (fingers along the forearm, palms facing in). The exact flange offset is inferred from the wrist mesh (±1 cm). Grasp centre 2.5 cm off the palm, 5.5 cm up from the hand base.
  • Reach = random uniform sampling of the 5 arm joints (waist yaw optional), 1 000 000 poses per arm, poses with self/ground collision removed (12–14 %). Volumes are voxel counts and grow slightly with more samples.
  • Kinematics only: no joint torque, payload or balance check. The rake mass bound comes from the 33 N·m elbow/wrist limits.
  • All coordinates are relative to the pelvis: x ahead, y to the robot's left, z above the floor.
